2. **Productivity and Profitability**: Agricultural productivity has historically outperformed most other industries in Australia, although there has been a productivity slowdown over the last two decades. Productivity growth is closely linked to profitability, with cropping industries showing higher productivity growth compared to livestock industries [4].

3. **Market Conditions and Prices**: There are expectations of falling real livestock production values due to a subdued economic environment and rising global supply. However, improved seasonal conditions have buoyed livestock and livestock product prices [1].

4. **Challenges and Risks**: Livestock industries face challenges such as biosecurity risks, including the potential introduction of diseases like lumpy skin disease and foot-and-mouth disease from neighboring countries. Additionally, there are concerns about the impact of avian influenza on the poultry industry [2].

5. **Export Markets and Trade**: The livestock industries are significantly affected by export markets, particularly the Middle East, which is a major market for sheep meat. Disruptions in these markets, such as conflicts and increased costs, can impact export viability and subsequently affect domestic prices [6].

6. **Environmental and Policy Factors**: Environmental legislation and policy instruments aimed at curbing greenhouse gas emissions from livestock agriculture could impact milk and dairy product production globally. These factors may influence the future of livestock industries in Australia [3].

7. **Industry Performance and Profitability**: The sheep industry is present in Western Australia and has historically been structured around wool production. However, recent years have seen a decline in sheep industry farm incomes due to unfavourable seasonal conditions and sharp declines in prices for sheep, lambs, and wool [1].

8. **Forecasted Trends**: There are forecasted decreases in cattle and calves production and values, as well as lamb and sheep production and values, due to lower prices and production volumes. These trends are expected to impact the overall livestock industry [1].
